After a car accident in Texas, **check for injuries, call emergency services when appropriate, move to a safe location if possible, exchange information, document the scene, and report the incident to the appropriate insurer**. In some cases law enforcement will investigate and prepare an official crash report. Exactly what an insurance policy pays depends on who was responsible, which coverages apply, the evidence, and the contract.

The first priority is everyone's safety—not deciding fault or negotiating a payment beside the road.

## First few minutes: protect people

If someone may be injured, request medical assistance and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Call **911** for emergencies. If the vehicles are drivable, nobody is injured, and it is safe, follow lawful directions for moving them out of active traffic. Turn on hazard lights where appropriate, watch for passing vehicles, and avoid standing in a dangerous traffic lane.

Do not move a badly damaged vehicle if doing so would be unsafe. For a hit-and-run, suspected impaired driving, injuries, or another serious situation, contact law enforcement. Avoid confrontations and never pursue a fleeing driver.

## Collect the information you may need later

The Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) recommends recording details as soon as practical. Exchange the following with other involved drivers:

- Full name, phone number, and other relevant contact details.
- Driver-license and license-plate information, as lawfully appropriate.
- Insurer name and policy number, or a clear photo of the insurance card.
- Vehicle make, model, and visible damage.
- Witness names and contact details, if they are willing to provide them.

Photograph cars, damage, traffic signals, road markings, weather conditions, and the surrounding intersection from **safe positions**. Write down the time and location. If an officer responds, ask how to obtain the incident or crash-report number.

Avoid posting personal insurance documents or another driver's identifying information publicly. Report facts accurately. A quick apology or roadside argument can be misinterpreted; allow investigators and insurers to evaluate the evidence.

## When and how is a crash reported?

Texas law requires an **investigating officer** to submit a crash report to TxDOT within **10 days** for covered crashes involving injury, death, or apparent property damage to any one person of **$1,000 or more**, under the relevant statute. **That is an officer's filing deadline—not a rule that drivers must file a TxDOT CR-2 form within 10 days.**

TxDOT specifically says it **no longer retains or distributes the old Driver's Crash Report (CR-2)**. For an officer-investigated crash, the **Texas Peace Officer's Crash Report (CR-3)** may be available through TxDOT's official crash-records purchase system to eligible parties. If local police give you a record number or other documentation, keep it.

Do not wait for a police report before notifying the insurance company when your policy requires prompt notice. You can explain that the report is pending and provide it when available.

## Which insurance company should you contact?

| Situation | Common next step | What to remember |
|---|---|---|
| You believe another driver caused the crash | Notify that driver's insurer and consider notifying your own. | The other insurer investigates responsibility and coverage. |
| You want damage to your own car addressed under your policy | Ask about collision coverage. | Deductibles and valuation provisions apply. |
| Other driver may be uninsured or leaves the scene | Ask about uninsured/underinsured motorist, collision, and other selected coverages. | Policy terms and any reporting requirements matter. |
| Vehicle cannot be driven | Arrange safe towing and secure personal belongings. | Confirm towing, storage, and rental terms before assuming payment. |

You may use a repair shop of your choice, although the insurer can evaluate repair estimates and covered costs. Read tow-yard and body-shop paperwork before signing. Keep receipts for towing, transportation, repairs, and other potentially relevant expenses.

## Hypothetical example: a minor collision in El Paso

*Hypothetical scenario:* Two cars collide at a low speed near a busy El Paso intersection. Nobody reports an injury, and both vehicles can move safely. The drivers exchange policy details and photograph the damage and intersection. One driver reports the loss to an insurer that day. Later, a repair estimate reveals damage hidden behind the bumper. The shop and insurer review the supplemental estimate; whether additional repair costs are payable depends on the coverage, assessment, and claim facts.

Do not assume the first estimate is the final amount or that a report automatically makes one driver legally at fault.

## After the claim begins

Ask for the claim number and adjuster's contact information. Maintain a simple log of dates, conversations, estimate changes, and documents submitted. Respond accurately to requests for necessary information and inspect the insurer's written explanation of any payment, deductible, or denial. If you disagree, ask what evidence or policy provision supports its decision and what review options apply.

Seek medical attention if symptoms develop; some injuries are not immediately apparent. Keep relevant medical and expense records. If an accident causes serious injuries or disputed legal responsibility, individualized legal advice may be appropriate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I contact insurance if the damage looks minor?

Check your policy's notice duties and consider discussing the facts with the insurer or agency. Damage may be more extensive than it first appears; a decision to file a claim is separate from merely asking about coverage.

### Do I need to send a CR-2 form to TxDOT?

No. TxDOT discontinued its retention of the old driver's CR-2 reports. Follow current law-enforcement and insurer instructions for your specific crash.

### Will an accident claim automatically pay for a rental car?

No. Rental reimbursement, liability payments, and uninsured-motorist benefits depend on the applicable policy and claim circumstances.
