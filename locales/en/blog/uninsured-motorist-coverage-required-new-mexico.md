Uninsured motorist coverage is one of the most frequently misunderstood parts of auto insurance. People may hear that New Mexico requires it, hear that it is optional, or see it listed on a quote without remembering making a selection. There is an important difference between a legal duty to **offer** coverage and a legal duty for every consumer to **buy** it.

**New Mexico generally requires a meaningful offer of uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage; it does not simply require every consumer to purchase and retain every offered UM/UIM option.** A policyholder may reject coverage when the applicable legal requirements are met. New Mexico's Office of Superintendent of Insurance (OSI) has also published important disclosure rules, including a **2025 requirement to show UM/UIM costs per vehicle**. Ask the carrier what was offered, selected, or rejected, and keep the actual forms.

## What is uninsured motorist coverage?

UM coverage is designed for certain covered losses involving an at-fault driver who does not have applicable liability insurance. Depending on the policy and New Mexico law, it may address eligible injuries and potentially property damage. Conditions and deductibles can differ by type of protection. The word “uninsured” does not mean every crash with a person who cannot be identified will automatically qualify.

Uninsured motorist coverage serves a purpose different from the liability insurance **you** carry for damage you may cause to others. Your liability limits do not, by themselves, promise payment for your injuries or vehicle damage when someone else causes a crash.

## What is underinsured motorist coverage?

Underinsured motorist (UIM) protection concerns certain cases where the responsible driver has liability insurance, but those limits may be inadequate for eligible losses. How the available UIM amount is calculated, how other payments interact, and what policy conditions apply are legal and contractual questions. Never assume the UIM limit automatically pays on top of everything received from another insurer.

Consider a simplified example. A driver causes a crash and has a liability policy, but the resulting covered injuries are more expensive than the available bodily injury liability limit. UIM protection could become relevant if it was purchased and the specific requirements are met. The presence of UIM coverage does not establish that a claim qualifies or determine the amount payable.

## Why the offer-and-rejection distinction matters

Some drivers believe UM/UIM is included because a salesperson once mentioned it. Others believe the state forbids declining it. Neither assumption should replace the declarations page and the policy record. New Mexico has developed specific legal standards around presenting UM/UIM selections and rejecting coverage; the details may depend on documents and current legal authority.

If you think you declined UM/UIM coverage, ask for a copy of the rejection or selection records. If you think it is included, check the declarations page for listed limits and premiums and confirm your understanding. A licensed insurance professional can discuss the options, while the written policy and governing law control the outcome.

## What changed in the OSI 2021 and 2025 bulletins?

These two bulletins concern *disclosure and informed choice*, not a promise that every uninsured-driver claim will be paid.

**Bulletin 2021-024: disclose important limits of minimum-limits UM/UIM.** Following the New Mexico Supreme Court's *Crutcher* decision, OSI instructed insurers to use disclosure and exclusion language addressing the limitations of UM/UIM protection purchased at minimum limits. The bulletin explains why that level of coverage can be misleading without an adequate explanation. It does **not** mean that a consumer can safely assume any UM/UIM limit produces a particular payment after a crash. Ask what the listed limit would mean in the kinds of losses you are concerned about.

**Bulletin 2025-013: offer and price UM/UIM per vehicle.** Following the Supreme Court's *Kileen* decision, OSI directed auto insurers to adopt forms disclosing the **premium cost for UM/UIM coverage on each vehicle**, so policyholders with multiple vehicles can evaluate selecting or rejecting it on a per-vehicle basis. OSI states that this applies to individual as well as commercial/fleet auto policies and provides suggested language for written selections and rejections. For example, a household with two insured cars should be able to ask what UM/UIM costs for **each car** rather than seeing only an unexplained all-vehicles total.

The legal validity of any rejection or policy exclusion depends on the full documentation, applicable law, and the facts. These bulletins impose compliance obligations on insurers; this article is not a legal review of a particular policy or a prediction about retroactive rights.

## Is uninsured motorist property damage the same as collision?

No. Collision generally concerns covered physical damage to your own vehicle from a collision, subject to its deductible and exclusions. Uninsured motorist property damage concerns a different coverage trigger and may involve determining that another driver was legally responsible and uninsured. Policy wording, claim procedures, available limits, and deductibles may differ.

For example, if a parked car is hit by an unknown vehicle, it is not safe to assume that UM property damage applies simply because the at-fault driver left. The insurer may have specific reporting and identification requirements. If collision is on the policy, it raises a different coverage question. Report the incident accurately and ask the claims team to evaluate all potentially applicable parts of the policy.

## Does New Mexico require everyone to buy UM/UIM?

The careful answer is that New Mexico law makes the offer and rejection process important, but it does not mean every policyholder must retain this coverage in every case. Drivers may be able to reject it in compliance with applicable requirements. Because procedures can evolve through legislation, regulation, or court decisions, verify the current rules with authoritative sources and your insurer at the time of purchase.

The ordinary **25/50/10** state minimum refers to auto liability limits. Do not confuse those figures with a universal statement about which UM/UIM options a particular customer chose. Also, **stacking** of limits, offsets, definitions of uninsured vehicles, and hit-and-run facts require more analysis than a short online example can provide; ask the carrier about the actual form and obtain qualified legal advice if a disputed claim raises those issues.

## Questions to ask about UM/UIM

- Is bodily injury UM/UIM included on this quote? At what limit?
- Is property damage protection included, separate, or subject to different terms?
- What deductibles, conditions, or exclusions apply?
- Was coverage selected, reduced, or rejected? What written record documents that decision?
- For a multi-vehicle policy, what is the separate **UM/UIM premium for each vehicle**, and how is selection or rejection recorded for each?
- Does the quote explain the particular limitations of UM/UIM at the minimum limits?
- How does the insurer evaluate a hit-and-run or a claim involving insufficient liability limits?
- How could this coverage interact with collision or other applicable payments?

## After an accident involving an uninsured driver

First address safety and emergency needs. If safe, collect the other party's information, the time and location, relevant photographs, witnesses, and any police report details. Notify your insurer as required by your policy. Do not make assumptions about whether a driver is insured without the insurer's investigation. Keep receipts and a record of communications.

Your insurer may investigate responsibility, other available insurance, injuries, vehicle damage, and the policy's conditions before determining if payment is owed. Filing promptly and giving accurate information can help with the process, but does not guarantee approval.
