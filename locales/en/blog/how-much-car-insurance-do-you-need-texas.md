The amount of car insurance you need depends on both Texas requirements and your personal financial situation. Meeting a legal minimum is not the same as having enough protection for every accident.

## Understand the Texas minimum

Texas generally requires at least **$30,000 for bodily injury to one person, $60,000 total bodily injury per accident, and $25,000 in property damage per accident** when using liability insurance to meet financial responsibility requirements. This is known as 30/60/25.

For example, the $25,000 property damage limit is the maximum available under that part of a minimum-limit policy for a covered accident, not an automatic payment. If the damage you are responsible for exceeds your available limit, you may be personally responsible for the difference.

## Questions to ask before choosing limits

- Could you comfortably handle expenses that exceed minimum liability limits?
- How valuable is your vehicle, and could you pay for repairs or replacement yourself?
- Is the vehicle financed or leased, with insurance requirements in your agreement?
- Who regularly drives the vehicle, and how is it used?
- What deductible could you afford after a covered loss?

## Consider coverage beyond liability

Collision and comprehensive may protect your own vehicle against certain covered losses. Uninsured/underinsured motorist protection, personal injury protection, and other options may address different expenses. Availability and selection rules vary, so review the quote and policy carefully.

## Avoid choosing on monthly payment alone

A lower premium can sometimes reflect lower limits, higher deductibles, or fewer coverages. When comparing options, line up the limits and deductibles before deciding which price is more suitable.

## A practical example of minimum-limit exposure

Imagine you are legally responsible for an accident that results in $40,000 of covered damage to someone else's vehicle and property. A policy with a $25,000 property damage limit would not simply expand to cover the entire amount. Depending on the circumstances and claim handling, you could face responsibility for costs beyond the available coverage. The example is hypothetical and not a prediction of a particular claim outcome.

This is one reason “legal minimum” and “financially comfortable” should be treated as different questions. Higher limits cost money, but may reduce the amount of uncovered liability you would face in certain events.

## Think about what happens to your own car

Liability insurance and physical-damage insurance answer different questions. If you cause a covered crash, liability can address the people you injure or property you damage, while collision may help with your own car. If the car is stolen or damaged by hail, comprehensive may be relevant. If the vehicle is essential for getting to work, consider how you would manage transportation while it is repaired, and whether any rental reimbursement option meets your needs.

## How financing can affect your choice

An auto lender or leasing company can impose coverage requirements under your agreement. That may include collision and comprehensive and limits on the deductibles you can select. Those contractual obligations are separate from Texas's minimum liability requirement. Before changing or removing coverage, check the finance agreement or contact the lender.

## A simple way to compare three quotes

Create three columns on paper or a spreadsheet. For each quote, record liability limits, uninsured motorist selections, PIP or medical payments selections, collision and comprehensive deductibles, rental and roadside options, total policy cost, and important exclusions. If any information is missing, ask before comparing. A lower price is meaningful only if you understand what changed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 30/60/25 enough?** It satisfies the standard minimum liability insurance requirement, but adequacy depends on your exposure and financial circumstances.

**Do I need more than the minimum if my car is old?** Your own car's age does not determine how much damage or injury you could cause to another person.

**Should I select the highest available deductible?** Not automatically. A deductible should be an amount you can realistically pay after a covered loss.

## How major life changes can affect the answer

A vehicle that you once used only on weekends might become your daily commute car. You might finance a replacement vehicle, add a teenage driver, or begin using a vehicle for business. Those changes can affect your insurance needs and may require you to update your insurer. Coverage discussions should reflect today's actual circumstances, not the assumptions made when the policy was first purchased.

## Review at renewal—and after important changes

A practical annual review takes less effort if you keep your declarations page and a short list of questions. Verify that all vehicles and regular drivers are represented correctly. Ask whether your limits still match your financial concerns, whether any exclusions matter, and whether your deductibles remain affordable. Reconsider physical-damage protection as the vehicle's value and financing change. A review does not mean changing the policy every time; it means understanding why your choices still make sense.
