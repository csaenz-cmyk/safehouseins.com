**GAP coverage may help address certain remaining loan or lease balances after a financed car is stolen or declared a covered total loss**, when the amount owed exceeds the car's insurance settlement value. It does not mean every unpaid loan charge will be covered, and it is not a substitute for comprehensive, [collision](/learn/collision-and-comprehensive/), or liability insurance.

The term **GAP** is often used loosely. Texas consumers should understand exactly which product a dealer, lender, or insurer is offering before paying for it.

## Why a financed car can be worth less than the loan

Cars generally depreciate with use and age, while loan balances decline according to the financing schedule. A small down payment, a long loan term, or financed extras may leave an owner owing more than the vehicle's actual cash value at a particular point in time.

If a covered accident totals the car, the auto insurer generally calculates payment based on the applicable valuation and coverage rules, less any relevant deductible. **It does not automatically pay the entire amount owed to the lender.**

## A numerical example: the loan balance is different from car value

*Hypothetical illustration, not an actual claim or quote:*

| Item | Example amount |
|---|---:|
| Outstanding auto loan | $25,000 |
| Vehicle actual cash value before applicable deductible | $21,000 |
| Illustrative comprehensive/collision deductible | $1,000 |
| Illustrative primary-insurance net settlement | $20,000 |
| Remaining loan after this settlement, ignoring other charges | $5,000 |

The simple difference between the loan balance and vehicle value is **$4,000**; the additional **$1,000** in the hypothetical net-settlement comparison reflects the deductible. Whether a specific GAP contract addresses any of the $4,000, the deductible, neither, or some other amount depends on the product's **definitions, limits, and exclusions**. The table does not predict a payment.

## GAP insurance versus debt cancellation products

TDI warns that a GAP product purchased through a **car dealer or bank might not actually be insurance**. Some are contractual **guaranteed asset protection debt-cancellation agreements**, and a different regulatory or complaint process may apply. The product name is not enough to determine what you bought.

Ask for the complete agreement and identify the provider, administrator, maximum benefit, termination terms, refund rules, and whether coverage is tied to a loan or lease. Do not assume a bank product has the same treatment as insurance regulated by TDI.

## What GAP usually does not replace

Read the contract for exclusions involving late payments, unpaid financing charges, extended warranties, past-due amounts, negative equity rolled over from another loan, or deductibles. Some agreements may have benefit caps or conditions about primary insurance. Coverage varies; the presence of the word “GAP” is not a promise that the entire difference will disappear.

Also distinguish GAP from **new-car replacement**, **loan/lease payoff endorsement**, and **replacement cost** options. The names may sound similar, but their limits, triggers, and eligibility can be materially different.

## When to reconsider GAP coverage

As your loan balance drops below the vehicle's estimated value, the financial gap may disappear. Check the agreement's cancellation and any refund procedures if you sell the vehicle, refinance, or pay off the loan early. An insurer's own GAP option may not be available for every vehicle or loan age, and a dealer product may follow its own rules.

The TDI recommends comparing offered GAP options and reviewing exclusions rather than assuming an option from the seller is necessary or sufficient.

## Questions to ask before agreeing

1. Is this regulated insurance or a debt-cancellation agreement?
2. Who administers claims and where can the full contract be reviewed?
3. Does the product pay any part of a primary-policy deductible?
4. Are overdue payments, warranties, negative equity, or other financed fees excluded?
5. Is there a dollar cap or percentage cap on the benefit?
6. What happens if the vehicle is sold, the loan is refinanced, or the balance is paid off early?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GAP required by Texas law?

GAP is not the state's standard minimum automobile liability requirement. A financing or leasing agreement may have particular terms; read them carefully rather than assuming every borrower must buy GAP.

### Does GAP repair my car after a minor accident?

No. GAP focuses on certain loan or lease differences after an eligible total loss, not ordinary repair bills.

### Can I buy GAP after purchasing the vehicle?

Possibly, depending on the provider, vehicle, financing age, and underwriting or contract rules. Ask about eligibility and review documents before paying.
