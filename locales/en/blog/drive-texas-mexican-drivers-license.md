**A visitor who holds a valid, unexpired Mexican driver's license may be able to drive a qualifying private vehicle in Texas under the state's foreign-license reciprocity rules.** But the answer depends on residency, age, license validity, permitted vehicle type, and other applicable conditions. A Mexican license should not be treated as a permanent substitute for a Texas license after a person becomes a Texas resident.

The Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS) is the primary official source for this issue. The rule about **permission to drive** is separate from an insurance company's decision about whether to insure a driver.

## How Texas recognizes some foreign licenses

DPS says driving privilege reciprocity may allow use of a **valid, unexpired foreign license for up to one year or until the person becomes a Texas resident, whichever comes first**. Its published guidance also contains conditions involving the driver's age, private-vehicle use, and applicable documentation. Some language on the general reciprocity page addresses international vehicle documentation; it should not be read as a universal requirement for every Texas-registered car driven by a visitor.

The [Texas Driver Handbook](https://www.dps.texas.gov/Internetforms/Forms/DL-7.pdf) describes certain nonresident driving privileges for people **at least 18** who are lawfully licensed in their home country and whose country recognizes comparable Texas driving privileges. Minor drivers and commercial driving situations require more specific attention. If your age or situation differs, consult DPS before driving.

| Situation | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Mexican visitor driving a private car | Valid license, nonresident status, driving reciprocity conditions, vehicle documentation, insurance. |
| Mexican license holder who moved to Texas | Texas-residency date and DPS license application requirements. |
| Person crossing from Ciudad Juárez to El Paso for a visit | Whether they are truly a nonresident and meet the applicable driving conditions. |
| Person driving for commercial purposes | Separate commercial licensing and vehicle-use requirements; do not assume private-vehicle reciprocity applies. |

## What changes when you become a Texas resident?

DPS states that **new Texas residents must apply for a Texas driver license within 90 days of moving to the state** to continue driving lawfully. The state has a separate process for out-of-country licenses; a Mexican license does not automatically create the same exam waiver that Texas offers under certain specific international license agreements.

It is important to distinguish **visiting** from **living** in Texas. Crossing the international bridge frequently does not, by itself, answer where you reside. Your actual home, duration and purpose of stay, and other facts matter. People who have just moved to El Paso should ask DPS how to satisfy its identification, residency, testing, and license requirements.

## Does an international driving permit replace the Mexican license?

Do not assume it does. An international driving permit is generally a translation or companion document, not an independent state-issued driver license. DPS guidance speaks to valid authorized licenses and documentation. If your license is in Spanish, consult DPS about the documentation it expects in your circumstances; do not rely on a website selling a so-called international license as proof of a separate right to drive.

**Getting insurance is not permission to drive.** A person without the legally required driving privilege must not assume that obtaining an insurance policy makes driving lawful. Only the applicable Texas DPS and other legal requirements determine whether the person may drive. Insurance eligibility and lawful driving are separate questions.

Different insurance companies may ask for different identification, license information, vehicle details, residence and garaging address, and driving history. An insurer's acceptance is **an underwriting decision**, not a legal determination that the person can drive.

Disclose the vehicle's actual garaging location and how it will be used. A vehicle primarily kept in Ciudad Juárez and driven into Texas presents different questions from a Texas-registered car mainly kept in El Paso. The territory in which a particular policy applies should be checked rather than assumed. A U.S. policy may have limitations involving travel into Mexico; Mexican liability insurance may be separately needed for driving in Mexico, depending on the circumstances.

**Never tell an insurer that a driver holds a Texas license if they do not.** A quote prepared with inaccurate residence, licensing, or vehicle information may not reflect the actual risk or available coverage.

## Two border examples

*Hypothetical scenario A:* Luis lives in Ciudad Juárez, visits his family in El Paso, and holds a valid Mexican driver's license. He should verify the applicable DPS nonresident reciprocity conditions and the coverage for the vehicle he intends to drive. His frequent crossings do not automatically make him a Texas resident, but neither do they exempt him from the relevant rules.

*Hypothetical scenario B:* Rosa moves from Ciudad Juárez to an apartment in El Paso and begins working locally. She should not assume she can use her Mexican license indefinitely. She needs to review the **90-day new-resident process** with DPS and accurately describe her new residence and where her car is kept to any insurer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drive in Texas with an expired Mexican driver's license?

The published reciprocity guidance concerns **valid, unexpired** foreign licenses. Do not assume an expired license is accepted.

### Is car insurance the same as legal permission to drive?

No. Even an active policy does **not** issue a driver license, excuse license restrictions, or authorize illegal driving.

### Can I use a Mexican license for a commercial truck?

Commercial driver-license reciprocity and commercial-vehicle rules differ from ordinary private driving. DPS notes specific recognition of certain Mexican and Canadian commercial licenses; verify your exact credential and operation with the appropriate authority.
