When water enters a business, it can soak flooring, inventory, electrical equipment, and records in minutes. The source of that water can be decisive for insurance. A suddenly damaged interior pipe, rain entering through a storm-damaged roof, sewer backup, and surface water flowing in from a parking lot **do not automatically receive the same treatment** under a commercial property policy.

TDI explains that many commercial property policies exclude flooding. Other water losses may be covered, limited, or excluded based on cause and contract. Business owners should understand these distinctions before relying on the word “water damage” in a proposal.

## A leak inside the building versus a flood outside

An interior pipe that suddenly bursts and damages insured contents may raise a different claim question than water that rises or flows across the ground from heavy rain. The first may depend on the policy's water-damage and accidental-discharge provisions; the second is generally a flood exposure. Coverage for either requires checking the exact definitions, exclusions, and circumstances.

Long-term leakage, seepage, poor maintenance, deterioration, and gradual moisture can be excluded or restricted. Rain entering through an existing opening may differ from rain entering after a covered wind event damages the building. Simply saying “a pipe leaked” does not establish a covered sudden loss.

| Water event | Main coverage question |
|---|---|
| Pipe suddenly breaks in office wall | Does the commercial property form cover accidental discharge and resulting damage? |
| Roof opening caused by covered wind | Do wind and interior-rain provisions apply? |
| Rainwater flows through door from flooded street | Is separate flood insurance needed? |
| Sewer or drain backs up | Is backup coverage included or specially endorsed? |
| Equipment slowly leaks for months | Are gradual seepage, wear, or maintenance exclusions relevant? |
| Mold develops after moisture | What specific mold limitations or remediation coverage, if any, applies? |

These are issues for investigation. No row is an insurer's advance approval.

## Flood coverage is a separate decision

TDI states that most commercial property policies do not cover flood and directs businesses to separate coverage available through private insurers or the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), depending on eligibility. The flood policy has its own insured property, deductibles, limits, definitions, conditions, and exclusions. Building and contents may need to be addressed separately.

The NFIP generally has a **30-day waiting period** for a new policy to take effect, but its official guidance lists **exceptions**, including certain mortgage transactions, qualifying renewals or coverage changes, and specific flood-zone or wildfire-related circumstances. Check the actual effective date and applicable program rules; **private flood policies may have different waiting periods and terms**. Buying coverage after a storm forecast does not guarantee immediate protection.

Do not assume that an address outside a commonly discussed high-risk flood zone cannot flood. The relevant question is the location's actual exposure and which policies can insure it.

## Mold and moisture need a specific review

Mold can involve cleanup, testing, lost use of premises, and contaminated contents. Commercial policy terms may limit or exclude mold, fungi, or remediation expenses. Whether mold follows a covered sudden water event or an excluded flood can matter. Review any sublimit, waiting provision, or special endorsement; do not assume all mold testing or treatment is insured.

When safe, stopping additional water entry and beginning appropriate mitigation can help limit physical damage. Keep records of when the problem was discovered, where water came from, actions taken, and any contractor observations. Work with qualified professionals where necessary.

## A hypothetical El Paso storefront example

*Hypothetical scenario:* After intense rain, water flows from a parking lot into a leased shop and damages $18,000 of inventory. The owner has a commercial property policy but no confirmed flood policy. Because the water arrived as surface water from outside, the owner should not assume ordinary interior-water-damage coverage applies. The insurer must examine the cause and contract; a separate flood policy might have been the relevant coverage if purchased and effective.

Another shop's interior supply pipe suddenly breaks, damaging cabinets and goods. That involves a different cause and policy analysis. The fact that both shops have wet inventory does not make the claims identical.

## If the shop must close after water damage

Property insurance for damaged goods does not automatically insure lost revenue. Business income or extra expense coverage, if purchased, may require qualifying covered physical damage and include waiting periods, restoration limits, and exclusions. If the underlying flood is excluded under the property policy, ordinary business income coverage may not respond to the related closure unless a separate provision applies.

*Hypothetical arithmetic:* If repair costs are $14,000 with a $2,000 applicable deductible, the simple difference is $12,000 **only if** all repairs qualify and policy limits and valuation rules do not change the settlement. A business shutdown or inventory loss is not automatically part of that same $12,000 calculation.

## Steps after finding water damage

Prioritize safety around electricity, structural instability, and contaminated water. Notify the insurer or agent promptly; photograph the visible condition if safe; document the water source, affected rooms, contents, and dates; and retain receipts for reasonable mitigation. Do not discard valuable damaged material without considering the insurer's documentation needs, unless safety requires disposal.

Ask for an explanation of any cause-of-loss or mold determination and keep copies of correspondence. At renewal, review roof maintenance, plumbing records, drainage, flood exposure, and whether sewer backup or equipment breakdown options are appropriate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does commercial property insurance automatically cover flooding?

Usually not under standard forms, according to TDI. Separate flood insurance should be discussed.

### Is a sudden pipe leak guaranteed to be covered?

No. It may be covered under some forms, but definitions, exclusions, maintenance facts, and other terms matter.

### Does flood insurance start as soon as I pay?

Not necessarily. Waiting-period rules and exceptions depend on the policy and program. Confirm the actual effective date.
