New Texas residents generally have **30 days to register their vehicle in Texas** and **90 days to obtain a Texas driver license**, according to TxDMV and DPS. Insurance must also meet applicable Texas financial-responsibility requirements, and some vehicles need emissions testing before registration. These are **different requirements with different timelines**—handling one does not automatically resolve the rest.

Here is the practical sequence for someone bringing a car to Texas from another U.S. state or from abroad.

## Start with where you actually live and keep the car

Tell an insurer your real Texas residential address and where the vehicle is **primarily garaged**. These may differ from where you work. Driving from Sunland Park or Las Cruces into El Paso for work is not the same thing as moving your residence to Texas; the actual facts matter.

Some insurance policies from other states may temporarily meet certain requirements, but do not assume that the old policy is sufficient for your new residence. Ask the issuing insurer what address, territory, and state-specific policy changes are required. Do not leave a known change of address unreported.

## The timeline at a glance

| When | Action | Official authority |
|---|---|---|
| As you prepare to move | Review your current policy and Texas insurance options; disclose the true garaging address. | Insurance company / TDI |
| Within 30 days after becoming a resident | Apply for required Texas vehicle registration; verify title and inspection paperwork. | TxDMV / county tax office |
| Within 90 days after becoming a resident | Obtain a Texas driver license through DPS; check any applicable testing/document requirements. | Texas DPS |
| Before registration when applicable | Obtain a passing emissions test in listed Texas counties, including El Paso. | TxDMV / DPS inspection program |

These are general new-resident rules. Special treatment may apply to certain military members, nonresident students, vehicle types, or individual circumstances.

## Step 1: update or replace auto insurance appropriately

[Texas's standard minimum auto liability amounts](/learn/texas-car-insurance-requirements/) are **30/60/25**: $30,000 bodily injury per person, $60,000 per accident, and $25,000 property damage. They are liability limits—not protection against every loss to your own car. If your vehicle is financed, a lender may require collision and comprehensive coverage.

When comparing options, give accurate driver and vehicle information, including the VIN, vehicle use, actual residence, and garaging address. State whether you regularly cross into New Mexico or Mexico. Cross-border territory and business use should be evaluated under the actual proposed policy.

## Step 2: confirm emissions and other inspection rules

Beginning in 2025, Texas eliminated the routine annual **safety inspection for most noncommercial vehicles**. However, eligible vehicles registered in the state's emissions counties still must meet the emissions requirements. **El Paso County is on TxDMV's emissions-county list.** Commercial vehicles have continuing safety-inspection obligations.

That means the right inspection process for a personal car in El Paso might differ from the process for a personal car in a county without an emissions program. An out-of-state or imported car may need additional identification or registration documentation.

## Step 3: register with the appropriate county office

TxDMV directs new residents to their county tax assessor-collector's office with appropriate records. Common items include the current title or out-of-state registration, **Form 130-U**, identification, proof of insurance, and applicable inspection information and fees. Confirm the requirements for a financed, leased, imported, or otherwise unusual vehicle before visiting.

Do not assume that transferring a title and registering a vehicle are identical operations. For some moves, a person may register without surrendering an out-of-state title, following TxDMV instructions. The county office can explain the applicable process.

## Step 4: obtain the Texas driver license

DPS handles driver licensing; TxDMV does not. New residents should review [DPS's moving-to-Texas guide](https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/driver-license/moving-texas-guide-driver-licenses-and-ids) for identity, lawful-presence, residency, exam, and appointment requirements. Certain out-of-state licenses and foreign licenses are treated differently for examination purposes.

**A current insurance policy and Texas registration are not substitutes for a legal driving privilege.**

## Two real-world-type moving questions

*Hypothetical example A:* A driver moves from Albuquerque to El Paso and keeps the same sedan. The driver updates the true garaging location, checks whether a Texas-specific policy is needed, researches emissions requirements, registers within the state timeline, and applies for a Texas license. The driver should not assume the New Mexico policy remains suitable just because its billing has not stopped.

*Hypothetical example B:* A person lives in Sunland Park, New Mexico but works in El Paso every weekday. Commuting to Texas is not the same as becoming a Texas resident. Insurance applications should accurately distinguish home address, overnight garaging, and workplace. Registration and licensing questions follow the person's actual legal circumstances, not the city where a paycheck is earned.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my old car insurance automatically transfer to Texas?

Not necessarily. Contact the issuing carrier or agent promptly to review the change in residence, state requirements, underwriting, and coverage effective date.

### Is the Texas deadline for plates the same as for a driver license?

No. TxDMV identifies a general 30-day vehicle registration deadline for new residents, while DPS identifies a general 90-day driver-license period.

### Does everyone in El Paso need an inspection?

Applicable vehicles need emissions testing under county rules, while most noncommercial vehicles no longer undergo the old statewide routine safety inspection. Exceptions and vehicle-specific rules exist.
