**Comprehensive auto insurance generally covers eligible hail damage to a vehicle, subject to the policy's deductible, limits, and exclusions.** A liability-only policy ordinarily does not pay to fix hail dents on your own car. Because storms can damage several vehicles in the same neighborhood, it helps to know what coverage you selected *before* severe weather arrives.

In Texas, hail can strike while a car is parked at home, at work, or outside a store. The important insurance question is not where you were standing when the storm started, but whether the vehicle, cause of loss, coverage, and applicable policy requirements qualify.

## Comprehensive versus collision versus liability

The Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) identifies hail as a typical peril under **comprehensive**, also called *other-than-collision*, insurance. [Collision](/learn/collision-and-comprehensive/) usually concerns vehicle damage caused by a collision. Liability concerns eligible injuries or damage to **other people** for which an insured is legally responsible.

| Coverage | Hail dents on your parked car? | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Liability only | Generally no | It does not insure your vehicle against weather damage. |
| Comprehensive | Generally potentially yes | Coverage, deductible, valuation, and exclusions apply. |
| Collision | Not usually the hail coverage | It addresses covered collisions, not simply falling hail. |
| Rental reimbursement | Only if selected and its terms are triggered | Not automatic just because repairs take time. |

Some insurers use different labels or special endorsements. Read the actual policy declarations and forms. Having “full coverage” written on an advertisement does not establish which coverages are active.

## The deductible can change the practical outcome

*Hypothetical example:* An adjuster determines there is **$3,200 of covered hail damage** and the policy has an applicable **$1,000 comprehensive deductible**. Under a simplified calculation, **$3,200 − $1,000 = $2,200** toward covered repairs. That does not mean the insurer must pay exactly $2,200 in an actual claim; exclusions, valuation, repair procedures, limits, and other conditions can affect the outcome.

If the eligible repair estimate is lower than the deductible, there may be no payment under that coverage for that claim. Compare deductible options with an amount you could reasonably pay after a storm; choosing a higher deductible may reduce a premium but increases what you may owe when filing a covered claim.

## What to do immediately after hail

First, stay safe and wait until the storm passes before inspecting the car. Hail and broken glass can injure people. Then:

1. Take wide photos of the car and close-up photos of visible dents, broken lights, or glass.
2. Record the date, approximate time, and address where the car was located.
3. Protect the vehicle against additional damage when reasonable and safe, keeping receipts for any temporary protection.
4. Contact the insurer or check the policy's claim reporting instructions.
5. Ask about inspection methods, repair estimates, chosen repair facilities, the deductible, and possible rental provisions.

Do not authorize extensive repairs based solely on a verbal assurance that an insurer will pay. Read repair authorizations and claim documents. If a windshield is damaged, ask whether glass receives different deductible treatment under your specific contract.

## Paintless dent repair and other repair methods

Minor dents may sometimes be repaired through **paintless dent repair (PDR)**, while larger damage, cracked paint, broken glass, or body-panel damage may require other methods. A repair specialist can assess the physical work; the insurance adjuster evaluates covered costs under the claim. You can discuss differences between a shop's estimate and the adjuster's estimate, including hidden damage found during repair.

Avoid assuming every small dent must be repaired or that a vehicle has been totaled solely because hail fell on it. A total-loss decision generally depends on valuation, repair costs, and applicable rules, not merely the weather event.

## Can a lender require comprehensive coverage?

If the vehicle is financed or leased, the contract generally may require comprehensive and collision. A lender's requirement is distinct from Texas's minimum liability requirement. Drivers who own their vehicles outright may choose whether to buy physical damage coverage, subject to availability and their own risk preferences.

## Hypothetical El Paso storm scenario

*Hypothetical scenario:* Marisol parks her sedan outside a workplace in El Paso. A fast-moving storm leaves small hood and roof dents. She has comprehensive coverage but does not remember the deductible. Before contacting a repair shop, she reviews her declarations, photographs the damage, and asks the insurer how the estimate process works. A neighboring coworker with liability-only insurance should not assume the same claim option is available.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will liability insurance pay for hail damage?

Not ordinarily for damage to your own car. Comprehensive is the relevant coverage to review.

### Does a hail claim always increase my premium?

No universal answer applies. Rating, underwriting, claims history, and state requirements vary. Ask the insurer how it treats weather claims; do not assume a specific renewal result.

### Does comprehensive pay for a rental car while hail dents are repaired?

Not automatically. Check whether rental reimbursement is part of your policy and whether its trigger, daily limit, and duration cover the particular situation.
