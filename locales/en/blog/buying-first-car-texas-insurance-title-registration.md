Buying your first car in Texas involves three different things: **insurance** for the risk of driving, a **title** establishing legal ownership, and **registration** for operating the vehicle on public roads. Getting one does not automatically complete the others. The process also differs depending on whether you buy from a dealership or a private seller.

This guide explains the order of decisions, what documents to request, and where to double-check the official rules before you drive away.

## Before shopping: determine the full cost of ownership

The advertised vehicle price is only part of what a car may cost. Budget for applicable sales tax, title and registration charges, interest if financed, maintenance, fuel, and insurance. The vehicle's model, repair costs, where it will be kept, its drivers, driving history, and the coverage selected can all affect an insurance proposal.

Ask for an insurance quote **before** agreeing to buy a particular car. A quote for one vehicle cannot automatically be applied to a different VIN, driver, address, or use. Confirm the effective date and time with the insurance company before assuming coverage exists.

If financing is involved, request the lender's insurance requirements in writing. Texas generally requires financial responsibility, but a lender commonly requires collision and comprehensive as additional protection for its collateral. Those coverages serve different purposes.

## Step 1: check the car and the seller

For a used vehicle, write down the **vehicle identification number (VIN)** and compare it with the title and vehicle itself. Use the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (TxDMV) [Title Check resource](https://www.txdmv.gov/motorists/buying-or-selling-a-vehicle/title-check-look-before-you-buy) to learn about potential title brands and reported history. A private inspection by a trusted mechanic can reveal mechanical problems a title check cannot.

Read the purchase agreement, any warranty terms, and the financing documents. In a private sale, TxDMV advises buyers not to leave without a properly completed title. Ask whether there is an existing lien and request appropriate release documentation when relevant. An apparently inexpensive car with unresolved ownership documents can become a costly problem.

## Step 2: decide what insurance you need

[Texas's minimum automobile liability limits](/learn/texas-car-insurance-requirements/) are **$30,000 per injured person, $60,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $25,000 per accident for property damage**—often written **30/60/25**. These limits address qualifying harm to **other people**, not repair costs for your own car. Higher limits may be worth comparing because a serious crash can cost more than the minimum.

| Coverage | Main question it addresses | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Liability | What if you cause bodily injury or property damage to others? | Does not repair your own vehicle. |
| Collision | What if your car is damaged in a collision? | Deductible, valuation, and contract terms apply. |
| Comprehensive | What if hail, theft, fire, or another eligible noncollision event damages your car? | Covered causes and deductibles vary. |
| Uninsured/underinsured motorist | What if an at-fault driver lacks adequate insurance? | Availability, selection/rejection rules, and coverage terms matter. |
| Rental reimbursement and roadside assistance | What extra transportation or help might be available? | Must verify selected options and triggers. |

If you already live with someone who has a policy, disclose household drivers and discuss whether you should be added to an existing policy or obtain your own. Owning a vehicle, holding a driver license, and being eligible for a particular insurer are separate questions. **Buying insurance does not authorize an unlicensed person to drive.**

## Step 3: understand dealership versus private-sale paperwork

**Dealer purchase:** A Texas dealer ordinarily files the title application for the buyer. Ask for documentation showing the title process and verify how registration and license plates will be handled. Texas replaced its former temporary paper dealer tag system with metal plates beginning July 1, 2025; follow the seller's current lawful process, rather than assuming an old-style paper tag is valid.

**Private sale:** The buyer generally must apply for a Texas title within **30 days** of the sale. TxDMV recommends going with the seller to the county tax office. The documents may include a properly signed title, Application for Texas Title and/or Registration (**Form 130-U**), lien release if applicable, government-issued identification, proof of insurance for registration, and applicable inspection information. Exact requirements depend on the transaction.

For a qualifying private-party purchase, Texas motor vehicle sales tax is generally **6.25% of the greater of the actual purchase price or 80% of the vehicle's Standard Presumptive Value (SPV)**, not the full SPV. Exceptions apply to certain transactions, and a timely certified appraisal may establish a different taxable value under the Comptroller's rules. This is **not** an insurance premium. Ask the county tax office about the actual tax and fees for your situation.

## Step 4: address inspections and registration

As of 2025, Texas no longer requires routine safety inspections for most **noncommercial** vehicles, but emissions inspections remain required before registration in certain counties, **including El Paso County**, for applicable vehicles. Commercial vehicles follow separate inspection rules. Requirements differ by location and type of vehicle.

Do not confuse a title application with an active registration, valid license plate, or proof of insurance. Confirm that everything needed for lawful road use is current before driving.

## A first-car example in El Paso

*Hypothetical situation:* Ana finds a used sedan listed for $9,000 by a private seller in El Paso. Before paying, she obtains the VIN, checks the vehicle's title history, orders a mechanical inspection, and requests insurance proposals using the car's actual details. She and the seller plan a visit to the county tax office with the necessary signed documents. Because the vehicle will be registered in El Paso County, she also checks the current emissions requirement for that specific car. None of those steps substitutes for a valid driver license or proof of coverage before she drives.

## A practical checklist

1. Compare total purchase and financing costs—not just the monthly payment.
2. Verify VIN, title condition, lien status, and vehicle condition.
3. Obtain a vehicle-specific insurance proposal and verify when coverage can start.
4. Identify lender requirements if the car is financed.
5. Confirm whether the dealer or you will file title and registration paperwork.
6. Check applicable emissions, identification, tax, and registration requirements.
7. Keep copies of the purchase agreement, title documents, insurance card, and receipts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I buy a car before I have insurance?

Buying a vehicle and legally driving it are different matters. Insurance is generally needed to demonstrate financial responsibility for driving and for title/registration transactions; financing may impose additional requirements. Confirm coverage and legal driving eligibility before operating the car.

### Does liability insurance cover damage to my first car?

Liability insurance generally addresses covered damage or injuries you cause to others. Ask about collision and comprehensive for damage to your own vehicle.

### Does the dealer handle everything?

Dealers ordinarily handle the title filing, but the buyer should still verify insurance, financing terms, registration, plates, and receipt of the required documents.
