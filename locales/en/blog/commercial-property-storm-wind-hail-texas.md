Storms can affect Texas commercial property in different ways. Hail may damage a roof or rooftop equipment. Wind may affect siding or signs. Heavy rain can reveal a roof leak or create surface flooding. These losses may happen during the same weather system, yet **their insurance treatment can differ according to the cause of damage and wording of the policy**.

TDI lists windstorm and hail among commonly covered causes of loss in many basic commercial property forms but emphasizes that exclusions and coastal arrangements vary. A Texas business owner should check the specific cause-of-loss form, roof terms, weather deductibles, and geographic rules before assuming a storm event is covered.

## Wind and hail are not automatically uniform statewide

Commercial property insurance is not standardized in Texas. A proposal may include wind and hail, exclude a specified peril, use a separate higher deductible, or include specialized roof valuation provisions. A business in El Paso should not assume its wording matches a building on the Gulf Coast.

TDI specifically warns that businesses in certain coastal areas and parts of Harris County on Galveston Bay may lack ordinary wind and hail coverage. The **Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA)** can be an option for eligible coastal properties under its rules. Eligibility, effective dates, wind coverage, and any related flood insurance requirements should be verified directly. TWIA is not a statewide substitute for every property insurance policy.

## The roof is a major source of questions

Roof age, material, installation, maintenance, previous repairs, and observed condition may matter when underwriting a commercial building. Policies may treat the roof differently from other property, including ACV valuation, roof schedules, cosmetic-damage limitations, endorsements, or special deductibles. None of these should be assumed without seeing the actual contract.

*Hypothetical example:* A small warehouse's covered roof repair is estimated at $24,000. The applicable deductible might be a flat amount or a percentage of the insured value; an ACV roof valuation could also produce a depreciation adjustment. Without those facts, saying “the insurer will pay $24,000” would be incorrect.

## How percentage deductibles work

A percentage deductible is commonly calculated using the defined policy basis, not necessarily a percentage of the repair bill. The following is **hypothetical arithmetic**, not a premium or representation of available Texas terms.

| Stated hypothetical deductible basis | Percentage | Deductible amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| $400,000 | 1% | $4,000 |
| $400,000 | 2% | $8,000 |
| $400,000 | 3% | $12,000 |

If a covered loss were $30,000, the simple difference after an $8,000 deductible would be $22,000 **only if** all other limits, valuation, and coverage conditions allowed. Review the policy's actual percentage base, because different contracts can define it differently.

## Wind-driven rain, leaks, and flooding are different issues

Rain entering because of a storm-damaged roof may be evaluated differently from rain that enters through an old opening or unrepaired maintenance issue. [Surface water rising from streets](/blog/commercial-property-water-damage-flooding-texas/) or flowing across a parking lot is generally a flood question. Most standard commercial property policies exclude flood, according to TDI, and separate flood coverage may be needed.

Do not assume all water that enters during a storm is insured under wind and hail. Cause-of-loss details, exclusions, building condition, and claim investigation matter. Photograph where the water entered when safe and document any visible exterior damage.

## An El Paso versus coastal Texas comparison

*Hypothetical scenario A:* A retail building in El Paso has a policy with wind/hail included but a separately stated hail deductible. The owner must review that deductible and roof valuation before assuming a payment.

*Hypothetical scenario B:* A Gulf Coast commercial building's property policy excludes windstorm. Its owner may need to examine a separate windstorm arrangement and eligible TWIA options. It would be misleading to transfer terms from one business to the other without checking both contracts and locations.

These examples reflect possible policy structures, not descriptions of actual Safe House Insurance customers or available offers.

## Practical prevention and documentation

Maintain roof inspection and repair records, keep drains clear, secure eligible outdoor equipment as appropriate, and document major improvements. Avoid climbing onto a dangerous roof after a storm; use qualified professionals and follow safety guidance. After damage, preserve photographs, contractor proposals, receipts, and communications with the insurer.

Be cautious of contractors promising that every roof will qualify for insurance payment. A contractor's estimate, even when detailed, does not determine covered cause of loss or claim amount. Ask about deductible responsibilities and avoid signing a repair agreement without understanding its terms.

## Questions to ask before renewal

Is wind or hail covered at this location? Does a coastal restriction apply? What is the actual roof valuation basis? Are there cosmetic-damage or wear exclusions? Is the wind/hail deductible separate? Does the policy insure attached rooftop equipment, signs, and outdoor property? Are business income and extra expense protections relevant if covered storm damage closes the operation? Is flood handled separately?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is hail coverage required in every Texas commercial property policy?

Do not assume identical statewide terms. Texas commercial forms vary, and coastal or policy-specific exclusions may matter.

### Does an old roof automatically mean a storm claim is denied?

Not automatically. Age, wear, covered cause, roof provisions, and claim evidence affect the outcome. The insurer must evaluate the facts and policy.

### Does wind coverage pay for street flooding?

Not automatically. Flooding is a distinct cause of loss frequently excluded from standard commercial property coverage.
